The Top Digital Marketing Trends for 2027 in South Africa

Your customers are online: about 51.7 million South Africans use the internet, 79.6 percent of the population (DataReportal, Digital 2026: South Africa). They find businesses on Google, on social media, and now through AI assistants. Here are the eight trends that matter for South African small businesses, with one action for each.

1. Be findable in AI answers

More customers now ask AI assistants for advice. They type questions such as "Where can I stay near Hermanus?" and get a short answer with a few names. Those answers come from public information on the web. If your business details are thin or out of date, the assistants pass you by. This is the new search frontier. We call this direction AIO, short for AI optimisation: the work of staying visible in AI answers. The same tools also help you work. They can draft a month of social posts, or turn one article into five shorter pieces.

Example: A guesthouse owner in Hermanus asks an AI assistant about her own town. The answer names a few nearby competitors, because their websites describe rooms, rates, and location clearly. Her own pages say only "cosy rooms". Clear, current information is the fix.

Action: Ask an AI assistant today: "Where can I find [your service] in [your town]?" Note whether your business appears, and what the answer says about you.

2. Win your area with local search

Google is where South African customers start. Google holds 91.75 percent of the country's searches (StatCounter, September 2026). For a plumber in Soweto, a burst geyser at night means one search: "plumber near me". The businesses with complete Google Business Profiles get the call. The profile is free. It shows your hours, service area, photos, reviews, and a button to phone you. An empty profile hands that customer to the next name on the list.

Example: The Soweto plumber completes his profile in one evening: trading hours, service area, photos of recent jobs, and a short description of his work. His listing now answers questions before the customer phones.

Action: Claim your Google Business Profile this week. Complete every field: hours, service area, phone number, and plenty of real photos.

3. Short-form video builds trust before the first call

Short vertical videos rule Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. They answer the question every customer asks quietly: what will I actually get? A before-and-after clip shows your work better than a paragraph. A quick tip shows that you know your trade. You do not need actors or equipment. A phone, steady hands, and good daylight are enough. Post regularly rather than perfectly. One honest clip a week beats a polished film once a year.

Example: A Durban tour operator films half a minute of a morning drive: the road, the guide's commentary, and the first sighting. That clip shows the experience more honestly than a price list.

Action: Film one short clip this week with your phone. Show your team at work, a finished job, or your product in use. Post it, then film the next one.

4. WhatsApp Business turns chats into customers

South Africans live on WhatsApp. Treated well, it becomes a customer channel, not a distraction. The free WhatsApp Business app gives you a business profile, a catalogue, quick replies, labels, and away messages. Customers get fast answers, and you keep your evenings. Speed matters here. An enquiry that waits overnight often goes cold.

Example: The Hermanus guesthouse answers booking questions on WhatsApp with saved quick replies for rates, parking, and check-in times. Guests get answers in minutes, and the owner clears the rest at breakfast.

Action: Install WhatsApp Business, complete the business profile, and save quick replies for your most common questions.

5. Email your own list, with permission

Social platforms change their rules and their reach. Your email list is yours. A first-party list, built from your own bookings, enquiries, and sign-ups, is the most direct channel you have. POPIA sets the ground rules in South Africa: ask permission, say what you will send, and give an easy way out of every message. Done this way, email builds repeat business instead of complaints. A sender address on your own domain, set up through our domains and hosting service, also looks professional and helps your messages arrive.

Example: A Johannesburg salon sends one email a month to clients who agreed to hear about specials. Every email carries a working unsubscribe link. Regulars book again, and the list grows with every new client.

Action: Start one simple monthly email. Ask permission at every booking, and add an unsubscribe link before your first send.

6. A fast website that works on a phone

There are more mobile connections than people in South Africa: about 127 million (DataReportal, Digital 2026: South Africa). Most customers will meet your business on a phone, often on mobile data. If your site is slow, cramped, or hard to use, they leave before they read a word. The basics decide the outcome: fast loading, clear prices, a tap-to-call button, and a short quote or booking form.

Example: An electrician in Pietermaritzburg has an old site that crawls on mobile data and hides the phone number inside a menu. Callers give up and dial the next result. A rebuild fixes both faults.

That rebuild is our Launch service: a complete, fast website, built and optimised for mobile. It goes live in 20 working days from complete content, for R12 000 to R50 000 once-off. See our website development service for the detail.

Action: Open your own website on your phone, on mobile data, away from the office wifi. Check how fast the home page appears, and how quickly you find your phone number. If either step frustrates you, it frustrates your customers too.

7. Reviews and trust signals win the click

Before customers contact you, they check what others say. Reviews sit beside your name on Google, Facebook, and local directories. A steady stream of recent reviews tells new customers that your business is active and trusted. Trust signals reach further than reviews. Real photos of your team and premises, a physical address, clear prices, and fast replies all do quiet work. Silence and stock photos raise doubts.

Example: A Durban tour operator sends every guest a short thank-you message after the trip, with a link to leave a Google review. The operator replies to every review, the glowing ones and the critical ones.

Action: After each completed job or stay this month, send one short message asking for a Google review. Reply to every review you receive.

8. Managed basics beat sporadic campaigns

Many owners market in bursts: a rush of posts before the season, then months of silence. Search engines, AI assistants, and customers all favour businesses whose information stays current. The winning pattern is dull and powerful: a website that works, profiles that stay accurate, a steady flow of content, and reviews coming in. Money matters too. About 90.9 percent of SMMEs self-fund their businesses (State of the SMME 2026, World Wide Worx, n=800). When your own cash pays the bills, a predictable monthly cost beats a large unpredictable one. Example: A guesthouse owner in Pretoria keeps a simple rhythm all year: current rates on the website, fresh photos each season, review requests after every stay, and a short monthly email. The guesthouse stays easy to find, because its information never goes stale.

Action: Choose a monthly rhythm you can keep: one email, a few posts, review requests, and current website details. Write it into your calendar, or hand it to a partner who does it every month.

The takeaway

You do not need to do everything at once. Pick two of these eight actions and do them this month. Most cost time rather than money, and each one makes your business easier to find and easier to trust. The basics, kept in order, are what keep a small business visible.
