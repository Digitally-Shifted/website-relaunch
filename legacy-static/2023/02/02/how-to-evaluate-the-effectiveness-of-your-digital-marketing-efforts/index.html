How to Evaluate the Effectiveness of Your Digital Marketing Efforts

Get more from your digital marketing by measuring what works. This guide covers the key steps: setting KPIs, analysing campaign data, and calculating your return on investment (ROI). Use the numbers to make informed decisions and grow your business.

  • Setting Campaign KPIs
  • Analysing Campaign Data
  • Measuring Marketing ROI
  • Conclusion

Introduction

Digital marketing sits at the centre of how South African businesses win customers. A website, Google Ads, email and social media all cost money every month. If you cannot measure the results, you cannot tell which campaigns earn their keep. An unmeasured campaign is money spent blind. The fix is not a bigger budget. The fix is a clear view of the numbers.

You do not need a data team to fix that. This guide walks through three steps any owner can follow: set key performance indicators (KPIs), analyse the campaign data, and calculate the return on investment. Follow them every month, and your marketing gets sharper with each campaign. For more plain-language guides, visit our digital marketing topic page.

Setting Campaign KPIs

A KPI is one number you watch to see whether a campaign works. The first step is choosing KPIs that match your marketing goals. A number that looks good on a report means little if it does not move the business forward.

If your goal is brand awareness, watch website traffic from social media referrals. If your goal is more enquiries, watch phone calls and contact form submissions. If you sell online, watch completed sales.

Keep the list short. Two or three KPIs per campaign are enough. Write them down before the campaign starts, and decide how you will record each one. When the KPI matches the goal, the data answers a real business question. Match one KPI to one goal. That habit keeps your reports short and your decisions simple.

Analysing Campaign Data

With KPIs in place, the next step is to analyse the data. Analytics tools such as Google Analytics show what happens on your website: how many people visit, where they come from, and what they do next. The tool is free to start with, and the basic reports are not hard to learn.

Two metrics deserve special attention.

Click-through rate (CTR) is the share of people who see your ad or link and then click it. A low CTR means the message is not drawing people in.

Conversion rate is the share of visitors who take the action you want, such as sending an enquiry or placing an order. A high conversion rate means your website turns visitors into customers.

Neither number stands alone. A campaign can pull many clicks, but if few visitors enquire, the website or the offer needs work. Look at the two numbers together, and your next step becomes clear.

Compare these numbers against your KPIs every month. The analytics and tracking chapter of our digital marketing guide for South African businesses explains how to set this up in more detail.

Measuring Marketing ROI

Data shows activity. ROI shows money. Return on investment compares the revenue a campaign generates with the total cost of running it. The formula is simple:

ROI (percent) = (revenue − total campaign cost) ÷ total campaign cost × 100

Worked example: a Pretoria plumbing company. Example numbers are illustrative. A plumbing company with 12 staff runs one Google Ads campaign for one month. The campaign costs R8 000 in total, including the ad spend and the management of the ads. It brings in 40 enquiries. The company wins 8 jobs, and the average job is worth R2 500. Revenue for the month is 8 × R2 500 = R20 000.

ROI = (R20 000 − R8 000) ÷ R8 000 × 100 = 150 percent.

Every rand the company spent returned R2.50 in revenue and R1.50 after costs. Anything above 0 percent means the campaign brought in more money than it cost. On these numbers, the campaign earns its place in next month's budget.

The same method works for any campaign, including paid advertising on Google or a social media push. Add up the total cost, count the enquiries and the sales, and run the formula once a month.

Be honest about the cost side. Include the ad spend, the monthly management fee, and any design work the campaign needed. A cost figure that is too small makes the ROI look better than it really is.

Conclusion

Measuring your marketing is not a once-off exercise. It is an ongoing habit. Review each campaign every month, compare the data with your KPIs, and calculate the ROI. Adjust the KPIs as your goals change, and put more budget behind the campaigns that prove themselves.
